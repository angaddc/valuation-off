Investors paid $852B for OpenAI and $965B for Anthropic in 2026, and both are now asking for more. This tool rebuilds those companies' futures from my model and lets you change the assumptions to see whether the prices make sense.

1 · Four futuresBust, Bear, Base and Bull, each with its own growth and profit path.

4 · Weigh the futuresMultiply each future's value by how likely you think it is, and add them up.
Before you start: what the controls mean
  • Chances of each future: your judgement. They don't need to add to 100; they are rescaled.
  • Base revenue and margin in 2030: how big and how profitable the "likely" future gets. Margin = operating profit ÷ revenue.
  • Discount rate: the yearly return an investor wants for waiting. About 10% for big listed tech; pre-IPO shares are hard to sell, so investors add a premium. At 14%, $1 earned in 2035 is worth about 31¢ today; at 10%, about 42¢.
  • Long-run growth: how fast revenue grows forever after 2035. It must stay below the discount rate.
  • Sales-to-capital: new yearly revenue each $1 of investment buys. Low numbers mean growth is expensive.
  • Value ÷ price: above 1× the company looks cheap against this model; below 1× it looks expensive.

A learning model, not investment advice. Revenue is on a comparable "net" basis (Anthropic's reported figures cut by 10%). Values are for the business; cash in the bank is only added if you switch it on.
